01

The pair

Two letters that share a stroke weight and nothing else. They are not forced into a ligature and not squeezed to match widths — a round G is naturally wide and a 6 is naturally narrow, and letting each keep its own width is what stops the pair looking engineered.

The G

Ring, a 60° gap on the right, spur and bar turning in on the midline — not tucked up under the top. Wide gap on purpose: it is the first thing to close up at small sizes.

The 6

A closed bowl sitting low, with a wide lazy shoulder rather than a tight hook. It stops short of the G's width, which is what keeps the pair from reading as two circles.

Kerned, not spaced

8 units between them — tight, because the G's open right side already reads as air. Equal metric spacing would have left a visible hole in the middle of the tile.

02

Where the warmth comes from

Warmth in this brand is not a colour choice, it is a geometry choice, and Design.md already states it: pills at 90px radius with 28–30px of horizontal padding, “over-padded by SaaS-default standards, deliberately so.” The monogram inherits that, not the palette alone.

Terminals are pill caps

Every stroke ends in a full half-round — the same 1:1 cap the brand's buttons use. No flat cuts, no angled shears. That single decision is most of the friendliness.

Counters run wide

The G's hole is 37 units across against a 17-unit stroke. A tighter counter would be more “designed” and would read as pinched at every size — the 6's is held open for the same reason.

The ground breathes

Design.md's peach–mist–green mesh sits behind the mark rather than a flat fill. It is the brand's stated way of making depth without shadows, and it keeps the tile from going dead.

03

Construction

Three strokes on one circle. Nothing is drawn by eye, and redrawing from these numbers gets the identical file.

  • Stroke12 on both letters, caps and joins round. One weight, no optical thinning
  • GRing radius 26 at (−25, 0), open −30° to +30°; bar on the midline in to x = 6
  • 6Bowl radius 15 at (36, 11); shoulder arc radius 42.11 from (7, −26), landing on the bowl’s leftmost point
  • Cap lineBoth letters run −32 to +32. The 6's bowl sits low so its shoulder reaches the same top
  • Kern8 units. Metric spacing left a hole; the G's open side is already half the gap
  • OpticalExtents are symmetric at ±57, so no optical shift is needed once the 6 sits at 36
  • Canvas-84 -84 168 168 — extent 114 × 64, a landscape band on a square tile
  • ColourGround #2451b8 surface-cobalt + mesh · mark #f1efe9 canvas-bone
04

The size ladder

True pixel sizes, and the honest part of this direction. Two letters inside sixteen pixels is two smudges — no kerning saves it. So the simplified cut drops to the G alone, drawn heavier, and the pair returns at 32. This is the cost of side-by-side, and it is a normal cost to pay.

Rules

Three that matter.

DoKeep the gap wide. Narrowing it makes a smarter G and a worse 32px.

Three unrelated marks ship today. A yellow bee in src-tauri/icons/ and public/app-icon@Nx.png, an amber hex cluster in public/g6.svg, and a raster gear in public/rust-gear.avif that Gear6Mark renders.
rust-gear.avif has no alpha — Gear6Mark rounds its corners specifically to hide the #e1ddd5 matte, and its own comment notes that currentColor tinting was lost when the mark went raster. The monochrome variant restores both, and 60KB becomes under 1KB.
The mesh needs baking, not CSS. The ground here is three CSS radial gradients. Export it into the 1024 master as pixels; an icon file cannot carry a stylesheet, and a flat cobalt fallback loses the depth the brand's own guidance asks for.
Squircle caution. The macOS tile is CSS border-radius: 23.5%, a rounded rectangle rather than Apple's superellipse. Generate the real .icns from the 1024 master with a proper squircle mask.
