Gear6 · shift gate, second pass

A gate needs
a stick in it.

Version one drew the slots and skipped the lever, so it landed as a hash mark with a dot beside it. Six revisions below, every one on the same blue tile so the glyph is the only thing being judged — plus the squint test that should have run first.

100 × 100 grid one ground, six glyphs tested at 30 px

Diagnosis

Why version one had to be explained

Version one

Three slots, a crossbar, and a filled circle where sixth sits. Correct as a diagram. Silent as a picture.

  • 01Four strokes at even spacing and equal weight is a grid. The eye files it as a hash, a table, a window — and stops looking.
  • 02The knob is a dot. A dot beside a grid is a bullet, not a lever, because nothing connects it to the mechanism.
  • 03Nothing in it moves. A gate is defined by a thing travelling through it; draw only the gate and you have drawn the least recognisable half.

Six revisions

Each one adds back a different missing half

Identical ground, identical glass, identical 100-unit grid. Where a variant dims part of the drawing, the dim part is the gate and the bright part is the thing you are meant to see first.

A · Lever

Recommended

The gate drops to 40% and a bold diagonal runs from the pivot out to the knob. That diagonal is the whole fix: it is the one line a grid cannot contain, so the shape stops being a hash the instant you see it. Knob and lever now read as one object.

Adds: the stick

B · Slotted Plate

The slots widen into channels a ball could actually sit in, dimmed like a recess, with the knob solid and riding in the bottom-right. Physical rather than schematic, and the heaviest silhouette of the six — which is why it wins the squint test below.

Adds: depth

C · Engraved Knob

Stop drawing the gate and draw the knob it is printed on — the shift pattern engraved on a cap, which is where most people have actually seen one. Recognised as an object immediately. Loses the pattern first when it shrinks.

Adds: the object

D · Bold

Version one with the stroke pushed to 13 and the knob grown until it merges with the slot end. Included as the control: it proves that weight alone does not buy meaning. Still a hash, only louder.

Adds: nothing — the control

E · Throw

The physically honest sibling of A: the knob travels along the crossbar and down into the slot, because that is how a lever actually reaches sixth. More accurate, slightly less legible — the route hugs the gate lines instead of cutting across them.

Adds: the motion

F · Six Positions

Every gear gets a dot; the sixth gets a big one. The count is now visible, which is the one piece of meaning the other five leave implicit. Also the busiest — six dots and four lines is a lot of incident for a 40px tile.

Adds: the count

The test that should have run first

At dock size, and out of focus

Top row is 30px, the size a Finder sidebar or a small dock gives you. Bottom row is 64px under a blur, standing in for peripheral vision — if a mark holds its identity blurred, it holds it everywhere.

Verdict

Take A, borrow B's stroke

A · Lever

The diagonal does the work no amount of extra weight could. It is the only variant where the knob belongs to something, and the only one that survives the blur as a distinct shape rather than a texture.

One change before it ships: at 40px and below, thicken the dim gate from 7 to 8.5 and lift it from 40% to 55%, or it disappears entirely and leaves a lollipop. B's channel treatment is the fallback if it still washes out on light wallpapers.
